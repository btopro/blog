Do more with less. It's been the ask of higher education for the last 15 years (at least), and in the age of AI, many think we may actually be able to do more with less, though studies are starting to suggest we actually just do more with the same and get more.

But without burning down the earth's resources, and without adding currency to the equation, can we as educators really do more with less? I am suggesting through a slight modification to how we teach and the outcomes we seek to achieve, that yes, we can actually do more with less.

The methodology

  2. Find an application leveraged by a university *that you can influence and improve*.
  3. Find courses and instructors who are teaching skills that could be leveraged to improve that application
  4. Slight modification to assignment scope to direct outputs to be towards solving those real-world problems
  5. Apply those improvements to the problem using solutions directly or indirectly from class
  6. Tell the story in class, illustrating the connective tissue between problems in the real world, classroom skills, and their part in creating a better world by solving a problem around them
