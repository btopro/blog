Do more with less. It's been the ask of higher education for the last 15 years (at least), and in the age of AI, many think we may actually be able to do more with less, though studies are starting to suggest we actually just do more with the same and get more.

But without burning down the earth's resources, and without adding currency to the equation, can we as educators really do more with less? I am suggesting through a slight modification to how we teach and the outcomes we seek to achieve, that yes, we can actually do more with less.

Stepping back in time

In 2015 I wrote down an idea for something called "HAX" on a notepad at a conference. By 2017 I had a proof of concept, by 2020 4 people working on HAX nearly full-time as part of their other duties, and by 2023, only 1 person on the project; me. Yet as you swipe in the image below, you'll notice that our productivity, visualized as rockets launching for major features shipped, hits pause around 2022, then explodes in 2024 through today!

What changed?

My teaching. I stopped teaching parallel concepts, and instead taught concepts that allowed for students to jump the tracks from learning about the web, to contributing to HAX while learning about the web. If you grab the slider in the image above, you can see that all of the 🔵 blue circles are things produced by the core development team without student involvement. All of the 🔴 red circles are with student involvement, contribution and development.

We started to ship more, with less, while actually educating and contributing more using the methodology I began to notice!

The methodology

  1. Find an application leveraged by a university *that you can influence and improve*.
  2. Find courses and instructors who are teaching skills that could be leveraged to improve that application
  3. Slight modification to assignment scope to direct outputs to be towards solving those real-world problems
  4. Apply those improvements to the problem using solutions directly or indirectly from class
  5. Tell the story in class, illustrating the connective tissue between problems in the real world, classroom skills, and their part in creating a better world by solving a problem around them

This leads us to name this process. Introducing the Sustainable Impact and Innovation Pipeline (SIIP). Taking a SIIP  (approach), HAX has been able to break through technical and academic barriers faced by previous open source projects coming out of higher education. How do you move an ocean? One drop or siip at a time. 😏

Visualizing this methodology

The more classes, faculty, and students involved, the greater the impact. I'll keep pointing to innovations in our community the next several months and just know that all of them are a result of the SIIP.
