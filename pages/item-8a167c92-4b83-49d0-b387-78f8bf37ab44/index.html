Hockey is a simple game that is difficult to play. Ice, movement, specialized equipment. But play it without nets and you can't envision how it works. Without lines and it's too easy to defeat. Play within the constraints, and it's a symphony of sport.

This is true of the web, as it is true of all games and tools. Without constraints, we cannot envision what is possible because at some level, the road must be set for us. Set it too much, we can't see past what's in front of us.

Set it just right, anything is possible.

Let's assume you've figured out why this is posted under "HAX" now. With the release of HAX 25, an important concept has been engineered under the hood that is not immediately visible -- constraints driven sites.

These sites make choices on your behalf. Yes, at first it's just 6 different types of sites, that are admittedly a bit half baked with content. But it's the shift that this represents that will bring us to Ubiquity that much more.

"Skeletons" are site settings + theme + content. This allows for a starting point for site building that is 100s of clicks down the road from our previous blank-only-templates. By being able to create a full working site, then export it to a "skeleton", you can effectively give someone else that site as a starting point.

Benevolent Restrictions

To coin a phrase from HAX Product Owner Bill Rose, we are working towards intentionally limiting the platform for your benefit. How is that possible? Envision HAX prior to 25, as the ice surface without lines, rules, etc. You can do "anything" but "anything" is difficult to envision. We need rules that help shape or understanding of what "anything" is possible.

The team is working now on additional restrictions now that 25 has put the capability in place. Here's things that you'll be able to craft via HAXcms in the next point release.

  • Removing buttons from the top of the UI when logged in as a site editor. Outline designer, insights, everything except edit pencil and Merlin can be removed
  • Removing buttons in the editor. view source, media, outline, even seeing all the blocks you'll be able to remove
  • Removing buttons from the editing toolbar. Taking the editing experience from 20+ buttons down to H1, H2, Blockquote, Bullet lists, Bold, Italic, Link. That's it.
  • Capturing this configuration + theme + settings + content to create an intentional experience that's still HAX at it's core

By removing capabilities, we can focus HAX to be a Medium clone, or a Ghost clone, or a Resume builder, or a Portfolio builder, or a Training builder; all while mixing within the platground of options we currently have. It's awesome you can create multiple choice questions, do you really need those in your resume? No. Being able to do so actually is distracting.

Most Portfolios, Resumes, and online trainings follow consistent patterns of content, editing, and blocks. So we can craft intentional experiences, benevolently, via these restrictions. You can still take your sites, remix them, put them anywhere, own every aspect of them -- but you'll be able to do so in a more intentional way. Less options, more focus, better results and envisioning what "anything" really can be.

The team is hard at work envisioning this future, where "skeletons" help shape people's perceptions of HAX more as a web-document production tool than a blank slate. You'll still be able to make blank slates, they just make more sense once you've been able to envision Club Websites, Portfolios, Blogs and more based on constraints that help you see what's possible.

One tool. Many use-cases.
